Living with a chronic condition and looking after your head

Endo, PCOS or another long-term condition taking a toll on your mental health? You're not alone, and support is out there.

Published 7 August 2026 Last updated 15 September 2026 2 min read

Sample article — this is placeholder content to show the layout. Delete or replace it before launch.

Living with a long-term condition like endometriosis, PCOS or adenomyosis isn't just a physical thing. Pain, fatigue, missed plans, endless appointments and waiting to be believed can all chip away at your mental health. If you've been feeling low, anxious or just plain fed up, that makes total sense.

Why it hits so hard

Chronic conditions can affect pretty much every part of life. You might be dealing with:

  • ongoing pain or tiredness that wears you down
  • missing school, uni, work or nights out
  • feeling like people don't get it, or think you're exaggerating
  • long waits for a diagnosis or treatment
  • worries about the future, fertility or your body

It's really common to feel grief, frustration or anger about all this. Those feelings don't mean you're not coping — they mean you're human.

Small things that can help

There's no magic fix, but a few things can take the edge off:

Find your people. Online communities and charities for your condition can be a lifeline, because talking to someone who just gets it is powerful. Be honest with your GP about your mental health as well as your physical symptoms — they're connected, and support like talking therapies can be part of your care. Pacing yourself and saying no without guilt are skills, not weaknesses.

You don't have to hold it all

If things are getting too much, reach out. Talk to your GP, use NHS 111, or contact Mind for information and support. If you need someone to talk to right now, Samaritans are free on 116 123, any time.

Your body is going through a lot, and so is your mind. Looking after both is not optional extra — it's part of fighting for yourself.
