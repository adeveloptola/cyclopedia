Why your mood shifts across your cycle

Unstoppable one week, under the duvet the next? Here's how your hormones can shape your mood, and how to work with them.

Published 21 September 2026 Last updated 21 September 2026 2 min read

Sample article — this is placeholder content to show the layout. Delete or replace it before launch.

If you've ever felt like you're ready to take on the world one week and can barely answer a text the next, you're not flaky. Your menstrual cycle is a whole monthly rhythm, and for loads of people with periods, mood rides along with it.

What's going on with your hormones

Your cycle is run by hormones, mainly oestrogen and progesterone, which rise and fall over roughly a month. These hormones talk to your brain, including the chemicals that help regulate mood, sleep and energy.

A very rough picture (everyone's different):

  • During your period: energy can be low at first, then start to lift
  • Before ovulation: oestrogen climbs, and lots of people feel more upbeat and social
  • After ovulation: progesterone rises, and you might feel calmer, or more tired
  • The days before your period: hormone levels drop, which can bring irritability, low mood or anxiety

None of this is a rule. Some people barely notice a change; others feel it hard. Both are normal.

Working with your cycle, not against it

Tracking your cycle alongside your mood for a couple of months can be really eye-opening. Once you spot a pattern, you can plan around it — maybe scheduling big social stuff for your high-energy days and giving yourself permission to rest when you know a dip is coming.

Things that can help during the tougher days include regular sleep, moving your body in a way you enjoy, eating regularly and being honest with the people around you.

When to get support

Mood changes that feel overwhelming, last most of the month, or make it hard to cope aren't something you should just put up with. Talk to your GP — it could be PMDD, depression, anxiety, or something else worth looking at. If you're struggling and need to talk right now, Samaritans are free on 116 123, 24/7.

Your feelings are valid, whatever day of your cycle it is. Get curious, start tracking, and be kind to yourself.
