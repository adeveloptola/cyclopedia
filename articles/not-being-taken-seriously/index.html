What to say when you're not being taken seriously

Been told it's 'just a bad period'? Here's how to push back, ask for a second opinion or referral, and get the care you deserve.

Published 15 October 2026 Last updated 29 October 2026 2 min read

Sample article — this is placeholder content to show the layout. Delete or replace it before launch.

"It's normal." "You'll grow out of it." "Have you tried paracetamol?" If you've heard these when you know something isn't right, you're far from alone. Lots of people with periods wait years for a diagnosis. The good news: you have more power in that room than you might think.

Phrases that help

It can feel awkward to push back, so here are some lines you can borrow:

  • "This is affecting my life — I'm missing school/work because of it."
  • "I'd like this written in my notes, please."
  • "What else could be causing this, and how would we rule it out?"
  • "If this doesn't work, what's the next step?"
  • "I'd like to be referred to a specialist, such as a gynaecologist."
  • "Could I get a second opinion from another GP?"

Calm, clear and specific is the vibe. You're not being difficult — you're being an informed patient.

Your options if you're still stuck

You can ask to see a different GP at your practice; many surgeries have someone with a special interest in gynaecology and reproductive health. Bringing a symptom diary makes your case much harder to dismiss.

It's also completely OK to bring someone with you — a parent, friend or partner can back you up, take notes, or just be a friendly face. And if you're unhappy with your care, every GP practice has a complaints process, and Healthwatch can help you understand your options.

Trust yourself

Pain that stops you living your life is not something you just have to put up with. If your symptoms suddenly get much worse, or you're worried and can't get an appointment, call NHS 111.

Being dismissed is exhausting, but your voice matters. Keep asking, keep recording, and keep fighting for your body.
