Period pain shouldn't stop your life

Some cramping is normal. Missing school, work or plans every month isn't. Here's how to tell the difference and what to do about it.

Published 28 September 2026 Last updated 20 October 2026 2 min read

Sample article — this is placeholder content to show the layout. Delete or replace it before launch.

"It's just a bad period." If you've heard that one, you're not alone. Loads of people grow up thinking severe pain is just part of the deal. Here's the thing: it doesn't have to be, and you don't have to put up with it.

What's normal-ish

Most people with periods get some cramping. It usually feels like a dull ache or spasms in your lower tummy, and might spread to your back or thighs. It tends to be worst in the first day or two of bleeding.

For a lot of people, things like over-the-counter painkillers, a hot water bottle, a warm bath or some gentle movement take the edge off enough to get on with the day.

Signs your pain might not be "normal"

It's worth getting checked if:

  • Pain stops you doing everyday things, like going to school, college or work
  • Painkillers don't really touch it
  • Your pain has changed or got worse over time
  • You get pain outside of your period, or during or after sex
  • It comes with other symptoms, like very heavy bleeding, bleeding between periods, or pain when you poo or wee

Severe period pain can sometimes be caused by conditions like endometriosis or adenomyosis. It doesn't mean you definitely have one, but it does mean you deserve to be properly listened to.

Make it hard to dismiss

Before an appointment, track your pain for a couple of cycles. Note when it happens, how bad it is out of 10, what you tried, and what you missed because of it. "I missed three days of school last month" lands very differently to "my periods are painful".

When to see your GP

If your pain is affecting your life, see your GP. If you have sudden, severe pain, pain with a high temperature, or you feel really unwell, contact NHS 111 or get urgent help. You know your body. Trust it.
