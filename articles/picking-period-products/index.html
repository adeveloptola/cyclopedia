Pads, cups, pants and more: picking period products

There's no right way to catch your flow. Here's a straight-talking guide to period products so you can pick what works for your body.

Published 2 November 2026 Last updated 12 November 2026 2 min read

Sample article — this is placeholder content to show the layout. Delete or replace it before launch.

Walk down the period aisle and it's a lot. The good news? There's no "best" product, just the one that suits your body, budget and life. Plenty of people mix and match depending on the day.

The main options

  • Pads: Stick inside your underwear. Easy to use, great if you're new to periods, and come in loads of sizes. Reusable cloth pads are an option too.
  • Tampons: Go inside the vagina and soak up blood. Good for swimming and sport. Use the lowest absorbency you need and change them regularly.
  • Menstrual cups: Soft, flexible cups that sit inside the vagina and collect blood. Reusable for years, so better for your wallet and the planet. There's a learning curve, so give it a few cycles.
  • Menstrual discs: Similar idea to a cup but sit higher up. Some people find them more comfortable.
  • Period pants: Absorbent underwear you wash and reuse. Brilliant for lighter days, overnight, or as backup.

Things to think about

Your flow, how active you are, whether you're comfortable with internal products, and how easy it is to change or wash things where you'll be. None of these products affect your virginity or your health when used properly, whatever you might have heard.

A quick word on TSS

Toxic shock syndrome (TSS) is rare but serious, and has been linked to tampon use. Follow the instructions on the packet, change tampons regularly, and don't use a higher absorbency than you need. If you suddenly feel very unwell with a high temperature, a rash, or flu-like symptoms while using a tampon or cup, remove it and get urgent medical help.

Can't afford products?

You shouldn't have to choose between period products and other essentials. In the UK, free products are available in many schools and colleges, and some councils, libraries and food banks offer them too.

When to see your GP

If you're getting through products really fast, leaking a lot even with heavy-duty options, or using products is painful, talk to your GP. That could be a sign of heavy periods or something else worth checking.
