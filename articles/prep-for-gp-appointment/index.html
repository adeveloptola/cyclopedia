How to prep for a GP appointment (and actually get heard)

Ten minutes goes fast. Here's how to walk into your GP appointment prepared, confident and ready to get what you need.

Published 11 November 2026 Last updated 11 November 2026 2 min read

Sample article — this is placeholder content to show the layout. Delete or replace it before launch.

GP appointments can feel like speed dating with your health. You've finally got a slot, you sit down, and suddenly your mind goes blank. Sound familiar? A little prep goes a long way.

Before you go

Spend ten minutes getting your thoughts in order. Write down:

  • your main concern, in one or two sentences
  • when your symptoms started and how often they happen
  • how they affect your daily life (school, work, sleep, sport, socialising)
  • anything you've already tried, like painkillers or the pill
  • any medicines you take and any family history that might be relevant
  • the questions you want answered

Lead with the thing that's bothering you most. If you've got lots going on, it's fine to ask if you can book a double appointment.

In the room

Be specific and be honest. "My periods are bad" is easy to brush off. "I'm off school two days every month and painkillers don't touch the pain" paints a much clearer picture. Nothing you say about your body will shock a GP — they've heard it all.

You're allowed to ask what they think is going on, what the next steps are, and what to do if things don't improve. If you don't understand something, ask them to explain it again. You can also bring someone with you for support, or ask for a chaperone during any examination.

Afterwards

Jot down what was said while it's fresh. Check when you should hear about any tests, and who to chase if you don't. If you don't feel listened to, you can book again, see a different GP, or ask about a referral.

You know your body better than anyone. Walk in prepared, and walk out with a plan.
