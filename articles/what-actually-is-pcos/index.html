What actually is PCOS?

PCOS is one of the most common hormone conditions out there, so why does nobody explain it properly? Here's the no-nonsense version.

Published 4 August 2026 Last updated 14 September 2026 2 min read

Sample article — this is placeholder content to show the layout. Delete or replace it before launch.

Polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS) affects roughly 1 in 10 people with ovaries in the UK. That's a lot of people, and yet loads of us only hear about it once a GP mentions it, usually in a rushed ten-minute appointment. Let's fix that.

The name is a bit misleading

Despite the name, PCOS isn't really about cysts. The "cysts" are actually small fluid-filled sacs (follicles) where eggs have started developing but haven't been released. Plenty of people with PCOS don't have them on a scan at all, and plenty of people without PCOS do.

What PCOS is really about is hormones. It's linked to higher levels of androgens (sometimes called "male" hormones, though everyone has them) and often to how your body handles insulin. Those hormones can stop you ovulating regularly, which is where a lot of the symptoms come from.

The three main signs

Doctors usually look for at least two of these:

  • Irregular or missing periods – because ovulation isn't happening on a regular schedule
  • Signs of higher androgens – like extra facial or body hair, acne, or thinning hair on your head (or blood tests showing higher levels)
  • Polycystic-looking ovaries on an ultrasound scan

Everyone's mix is different. You might have super-irregular periods and nothing else, or clockwork-ish cycles and stubborn acne.

It's common, manageable and not your fault

Nobody knows exactly what causes PCOS, but it often runs in families. It's not caused by anything you did. There's no cure, but symptoms can be managed with things like lifestyle tweaks, hormonal treatments or other medication, depending on what bothers you most and what you want for your body.

When to see your GP

If your periods are irregular, have stopped, or you're dealing with symptoms like excess hair or acne that are getting you down, book a GP appointment. You deserve answers, and you're allowed to ask for them.
