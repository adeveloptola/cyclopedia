What's a "normal" period anyway?

How long, how heavy, how often? Normal covers a lot of ground. Here's what's typical and the signs that something's worth checking.

Published 20 October 2026 Last updated 20 October 2026 2 min read

Sample article — this is placeholder content to show the layout. Delete or replace it before launch.

Here's the truth: there's no single "normal" period. Bodies vary loads. But there is a typical range, and knowing it helps you spot when something's off.

The typical range

  • How often: A cycle usually lasts between about 21 and 35 days, counting from the first day of one period to the first day of the next.
  • How long: Bleeding usually lasts somewhere between two and seven days.
  • How much: Most people lose a few tablespoons of blood over a whole period. It often looks like more than it is.
  • Colour: Bright red, dark red, brown — all usually fine. Small clots can be normal too, especially on heavier days.

In the first couple of years after periods start, cycles are often irregular while your hormones settle down. Stress, illness, big changes in weight or exercise, and some contraception can also shift things around.

Signs it might be heavy

Heavy periods are really common, and they're not something you just have to cope with. Your periods might be heavy if you:

  • Need to change your pad or tampon every hour or two, or empty your cup more often than usual
  • Have to use two types of product together
  • Pass clots larger than about 2.5cm
  • Bleed through onto your clothes or bedding
  • Feel tired, dizzy or short of breath, which can be a sign of anaemia

Know your normal

The most useful "normal" is your own. Tracking your periods for a few months gives you a baseline, so you'll notice if something changes. Changes are often the most important clue.

When to see your GP

See your GP if your periods are heavy, very painful, irregular, have stopped (and you're not pregnant), or you bleed between periods or after sex. If you bleed really heavily and feel faint or unwell, contact NHS 111 or get urgent help.
