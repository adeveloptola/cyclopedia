Why does an endometriosis diagnosis take so long?

On average it takes around eight years to get an endometriosis diagnosis in the UK. Here's why, and how to push for answers sooner.

Published 10 November 2026 Last updated 10 November 2026 2 min read

Sample article — this is placeholder content to show the layout. Delete or replace it before launch.

Around eight years. That's roughly how long it takes, on average, to get an endometriosis diagnosis in the UK. Eight years of pain, appointments and being told it's "probably nothing". It's not good enough, and knowing why it happens can help you cut through it.

Why it takes so long

There's no single reason. It's more of a pile-up:

  • Period pain is normalised. Lots of people (and some professionals) still think severe pain is just part of having periods.
  • Symptoms overlap with other conditions like IBS or bladder problems, so endo gets missed.
  • Scans don't always show it. An ultrasound or MRI can spot some endometriosis, but a normal scan doesn't rule it out.
  • The most reliable way to confirm it is surgery, usually a laparoscopy (keyhole surgery), which means waiting for a specialist referral.
  • People wait to speak up because they've been told for years their pain is normal.

How to push for answers

You can't control waiting lists, but you can make your case as clear as possible:

  • Keep a symptom diary covering pain, bleeding, bowel and bladder symptoms, and how it affects your life.
  • Use the word "endometriosis" and ask directly whether it could be the cause.
  • Ask what the next step is if a treatment doesn't work, and when you should come back.
  • Ask about a referral to a gynaecologist if your symptoms continue.
  • Bring someone with you if it helps you feel more confident.

You're also allowed to ask to see a different GP. That's not rude, it's your right.

You don't always need a diagnosis to get help

Your GP can often start treatments for symptoms, like pain relief or hormonal options, while you're waiting for tests. Ask about this.

When to see your GP

If you've got symptoms of endometriosis, see your GP, and go back if things don't improve. Persistence isn't being a nuisance. It's how a lot of people finally get diagnosed.
