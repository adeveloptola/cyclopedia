Your cycle's four phases, explained

Your cycle is way more than the days you bleed. Meet the four phases and why you might feel different in each one.

Published 14 September 2026 Last updated 6 October 2026 2 min read

Sample article — this is placeholder content to show the layout. Delete or replace it before launch.

Your period gets all the attention, but it's actually just one part of a whole cycle. Understanding what's going on the rest of the month can help you make sense of your energy, mood, skin and more.

A cycle runs from day one of your period to the day before the next one. The average is around 28 days, but anywhere from about 21 to 35 days is common, and it can be less regular in the first few years after periods start.

The four phases

  • Menstrual phase (your period): The lining of your womb sheds and you bleed, usually for around two to seven days. Hormone levels are low, so you might feel tired or crampy.
  • Follicular phase: This actually starts on day one too and overlaps with your period. Your body gets an egg ready and oestrogen starts rising. Lots of people notice their energy and mood picking up.
  • Ovulation: An egg is released, often around the middle of your cycle, though timing varies. Some people notice clearer, stretchy discharge or a twinge of pain on one side.
  • Luteal phase: After ovulation, progesterone rises to prepare the womb lining. If you don't get pregnant, hormone levels drop, and that drop can bring PMS symptoms like bloating, sore boobs, spots or mood changes before your period starts again.

Why it's worth tracking

Tracking your cycle, whether with an app, a calendar or a notes page, helps you spot patterns. You might notice you always feel low a few days before your period, or get migraines at a certain point. That's useful info for planning your life and for any chat with a doctor.

Everyone's different

Not everyone feels big shifts between phases, and that's fine too. Hormonal contraception can also change or pause this pattern.

When to see your GP

If your cycle is regularly shorter than 21 days or longer than 35, your periods stop, or PMS is seriously affecting your life, have a chat with your GP.
